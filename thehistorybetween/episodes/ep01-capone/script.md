> VO. Present tense, calm, unhurried. On screen: `[brackets]` = visual cue.

`[Black. Typewriter date stamp: CHICAGO — 14 FEBRUARY 1929 — 10:30 AM]`

It’s a freezing Thursday morning in Chicago. February 14th, 1929.

`[Snowy street, 1920s Chicago. Slow push-in.]`

A black Cadillac turns onto North Clark Street. It has a siren, a gong, and a rack for rifles. To anyone watching, it’s a police car.

`[Map: Chicago → Lincoln Park → pin drops on 2122 N. Clark St.]`

It stops outside a brick garage at number 2122. The sign out front says S-M-C Cartage Company. But nobody here moves furniture. This is a depot for the North Side Gang, the last crew in Chicago still standing in the way of one man.

`[Garage exterior. Four silhouettes step out of the car.]`

Four men get out. Two of them are wearing police uniforms.

`[Inside the garage: dim, a truck, seven figures in hats and overcoats, backs turned.]`

Inside, seven men are waiting, reportedly for a delivery of hijacked whiskey. When they see the uniforms, nobody panics. In Prohibition Chicago, raids happen all the time. Usually it means a fine. Maybe a bribe. So they do what they’re told. They line up facing the back wall.

`[Hard cut to black. Single line of text: “~70 rounds.”]`

Then the officers open fire. Two Thompson submachine guns. Two shotguns. Around seventy rounds in a matter of seconds.

`[Quote card: “Nobody shot me.” — Frank Gusenberg]`

When real police arrive, six men are dead. One is still breathing. Frank Gusenberg has fourteen bullets in him. A detective asks him who did it.

“Nobody shot me,” he says.

Three hours later, he’s dead too.

`[Map pull-out: Chicago → Florida. Line drawn ~1,400 miles, label “Miami”.]`

The man everyone suspects is more than a thousand miles away, at his estate in Florida. He has the perfect alibi.

`[Silhouette portrait. Name types on: ALPHONSE CAPONE — AGE 30]`

His name is Alphonse Capone. He’s thirty years old, and he’s the most famous gangster in America.

`[Newspaper headlines stacking up.]`

The massacre makes headlines around the world. And it forces a question the government can no longer ignore: how do you catch a man who never pulls the trigger himself?

`[Ledger page, columns of numbers, slow drift.]`

The answer will take a Treasury agent, a nervous bookkeeper, a few forgotten ledgers… and the most boring crime in the American legal code.

`[Title card: THE HUNT FOR AL CAPONE]`

This is the story of the hunt for Al Capone.

## FULL EPISODE OUTLINE

### Chapter 1 — Brooklyn (~2:00)
- Born 17 Jan 1899, Brooklyn, to Italian immigrant parents. Leaves school young.
- The scar: slashed in a Coney Island bar fight. This is where the “Scarface” nickname comes from, and he hated it.
- Mentor **Johnny Torrio**, a Brooklyn racketeer who moves to Chicago in 1909.

### Chapter 2 — Chicago (~3:00)
- **17 Jan 1920:** Prohibition takes effect on Capone’s **21st birthday** *(short-form hook)*.
- Torrio invites him to Chicago (~1920) to work for “Big Jim” Colosimo; Colosimo is murdered in 1920 and Torrio takes over.
- 1925: Torrio is shot, survives, retires, and hands the empire to Capone, now 26.
- **Beat list (fern-style “Strategy one…six”):**
  1. *The beer economy:* breweries, speakeasies, distribution.
  2. *Buying the city:* police, judges, City Hall.
  3. *Cicero:* a whole suburb as headquarters.
  4. *Image:* public celebrity, and later a soup kitchen in the Depression.
  5. *Violence:* the beer wars with the North Side Gang (O’Banion → Weiss → Moran).
  6. *Insulation:* never signs anything, never owns anything, and keeps no bank accounts in his own name.

### Chapter 3 — 2122 North Clark (~2:30)
- Back to the massacre: what actually happened, Bugs Moran arriving late, seeing the “police” car and walking away.
- **Calvin Goddard:** ballistics shows the guns weren’t police weapons. The .45 bullets are matched to two Thompsons found at hitman Fred “Killer” Burke’s home in Michigan. The case leads to one of America’s first independent crime labs *(short-form hook)*.
- No one is ever convicted of the massacre.

### Chapter 4 — Washington (~3:00)
- President Hoover, inaugurated three weeks after the massacre, wants Capone dealt with.
- **The legal key:** *US v. Sullivan* (1927). The Supreme Court rules that illegal income is still taxable.
- Two federal tracks:
  - **Prohibition Bureau:** Eliot Ness and the “Untouchables” raid breweries. Loud and public, it costs Capone money.
  - **Treasury Special Intelligence Unit:** Elmer Irey and agent **Frank J. Wilson** quietly build a tax case.
- **April 1930:** the Chicago Crime Commission publishes its first “Public Enemies” list, with Capone at No. 1.
- The **“Secret Six”**: Chicago businessmen who privately fund the fight.
- Practice run: Capone’s brother **Ralph** is convicted of tax evasion in 1930.

### Chapter 5 — The Ledger (~3:00)
- Wilson finds seized, mislabeled ledgers from a gambling house, with entries like *“Frank paid $17,500 for Al.”*
- He tracks down the bookkeeper, **Leslie Shumway**, who is persuaded to testify.
- June 1931: indictment. Capone expects a plea deal; Judge **James Wilkerson** refuses to be bound by it.
- Trial: Wilkerson swaps the entire jury panel with one from another courtroom to stop tampering *(short-form hook)*.
- **17 Oct 1931:** guilty on several counts after ~9 hours of deliberation. **24 Oct:** sentenced to **11 years**, a $50,000 fine and ~$30,000 in court costs.

### Ending — Alcatraz (~1:30)
- Atlanta federal prison, then Alcatraz (1934). Syphilis takes his mind.
- Released in 1939; dies in Florida on 25 Jan 1947, aged 48.
- **Loop back:** the garage at 2122 N. Clark is demolished in 1967. The most violent man in America was never convicted of a single killing. A ledger brought him down, not a gun.
- Close: the history that happens in between, in the paperwork, the bookkeepers and the boring details.
