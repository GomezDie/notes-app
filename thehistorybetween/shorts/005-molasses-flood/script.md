# 005 The wave of molasses

**Hook (on screen):** A WAVE OF MOLASSES KILLED 21 PEOPLE
**Length:** 66.7 s (narration 64.7 s). Credits: ~9,002.
**Series:** Standalone (first non-Capone Short, per the two-per-topic rule)

## Narration
Boston, January fifteenth, nineteen nineteen. Just after noon, people in the North End hear a deep rumble. Then a sound like machine-gun fire. It's rivets, bursting out of a steel tank holding more than two million gallons of molasses. The tank splits open. A brown wave, by some accounts twenty-five feet high, rolls through the streets at around thirty-five miles an hour. It flattens buildings. It knocks a firehouse off its foundation. People and horses are swept up and stuck. As the winter air cools it, the molasses thickens, and anyone trapped can't break free. Twenty-one people die, including children. About a hundred and fifty are hurt. The company blames anarchists with a bomb. But the tank had leaked since the day it was filled. Neighbors collected the drips in buckets. The company's fix? It painted the tank brown. In nineteen twenty-five, a court auditor rules: no bomb. Just a bad tank, painted to hide its leaks. Follow The History Between for more.
